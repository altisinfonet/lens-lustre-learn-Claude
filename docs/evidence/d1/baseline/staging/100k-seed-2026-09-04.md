## ⚠ THE 100,000 ROWS STAY ON STAGING. THAT IS THE DESIGN, NOT AN OVERSIGHT.

Phase 7 re-measures against **the same thousand-hundred rows**, and a measurement
taken at 100k on Tuesday is only comparable with one repeated later if the rows
are still there. P20's search-latency budget, P29's N+1 trace and P34's policy
and index costs are all unprovable at 17 posts; that is why the seeder exists.

They are **reversible**, and the reversal is proven rather than asserted: the
teardown was demonstrated on a 300-row seed at `122d6ea` (F-79/F-80), removing
the posts, the 513 trigger-caused `user_notifications` rows, and returning every
one of the five counts to its pre-seed value with **zero residue on the derived
id set**. `node scripts/db-seed-staging.mjs --teardown --yes --rows 100000`
removes exactly this set and nothing else, whenever the Owner asks.

`posts_total` 100,017 = **17 member posts + 100,000 seeded**. The 17 are
untouched; the seed only ever inserts rows whose id is
`md5('50mm-seed-v1:post:' || n)::uuid`.

### The three tables the seed cannot touch did not move

`profiles` 513, `follows` 513, `post_media` 5 — identical at 15:54:48Z and at
16:39:06Z, across a failed run, a partial state, and a resume. This is the
confinement half of the teardown verdict (F-80) applied to the seed itself:
`user_notifications` rising is the fan-out working; a member table moving would
be damage.

### user_notifications: +74,898, and it is expected

`trg_fan_out_new_post` writes one row per follower for every PUBLIC post. At the
default 80/10/10 privacy mix and 513 follow edges that is ~0.75 rows per seeded
post. Every one of them is reachable by the derived id set through
`reference_id`, which is what F-79 added, and comes out with the teardown.

### The intermediate state, recorded rather than smoothed over

The first attempt died at 80,200 rows (F-84 — `statement_timeout` was set in
PGOPTIONS, which the session pooler strips, so the server's 120 s applied instead
of the requested 600 s). The partial seed was left in place and **resumed**, not
torn down and restarted: `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING` makes the run
re-entrant, so ordinals 1–80,200 were no-ops and 80,201–100,000 were written.
Two independent readings of that intermediate state — mine at 16:08:57Z and the
Auditor's at 16:13:27Z — agreed on every figure.

The resume ran **189 s in a single seeding step**, clearing the 120 s limit that
had killed the previous attempt. That is the F-84 fix proven on the real
transport, not only on the fixture.

### database_size 217 MB → 577 MB

Includes dead tuples from the 300-row teardown earlier in the day awaiting
autovacuum; Postgres does not return pages to the OS on DELETE. The row counts
and the residue probes, not the size, are what answer "what is present".

## The baseline JSON is NOT in this commit

`scripts/db-baseline.mjs` ran successfully after the seed (run **33896446960**,
file `baseline-2026-09-04T16-41-24-707Z.json`,
sha256 `598d37529663d88e8b4cd7032af9cb88fc11a02cd816bc68e1145751ca984844`), and
its own read-only negative control passed — so the F-78 wrapper holds on the real
transport too.

It could not be committed, and the reason is recorded in this same PR: see
**0-D1-01b** in `.github/workflows/d1-baseline.yml`. Both routes to the file
failed for one artefact, in ways neither had been tested against:

1. the artifact host answers **403** to this environment's egress proxy — twice
   the same afternoon, by organisation policy, with no per-tool remedy;
2. the log route emitted the file as **raw base64**, and at ~1 MB that is roughly
   **13,200 log lines** — about 1.7 MB to carry out and write back. I had proved
   that route on a **37,437-byte** stand-in, 26× too small.

The emitter now gzips first — measured on an **1,854,281-byte** stand-in: **317
base64 lines instead of ~24,723**, round trip byte-identical, and one corrupted
character makes `gunzip` refuse the stream. The JSON will be committed from the
next baseline run once that change is on staging.
